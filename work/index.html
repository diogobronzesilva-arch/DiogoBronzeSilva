Work

Sales, technology, decisions, and the human part of the job.

I've spent more than a decade in B2B software sales. The tools change. The difficult part remains remarkably human.

Sales is conversation

I have worked across different markets, company sizes and sales motions, from smaller businesses to larger and more complex accounts. The products, processes and acronyms change, but the part that matters most rarely does: two people trying to understand a problem well enough to decide what to do next.

Good process helps. A clean CRM helps. Preparation helps. Methodologies can help. But none of them are the sale itself. The sale happens in the conversation, in the quality of the questions, in whether someone feels understood, and in whether the next decision becomes clearer than it was before.

Good sales helps people decide

I don't think the job is to push someone towards the outcome that suits the seller. The job is to help make the decision legible: what changes if we do this, what happens if we don't, what matters now, what can wait, and what would make this a bad decision.

That still involves persuasion. It should. But persuasion and pressure are not the same thing. Good sales helps people decide. It doesn't decide for them.

AI around the conversation

AI is now part of how I work every day. I use it to prepare, research, structure information, review pipeline, sharpen follow-ups and reduce the administrative work that gathers around a sales process.

The useful question, for me, is not how much of selling can be automated. It is how much unnecessary work can be removed so that more attention is available for the part that should not be automated.

AI should remove the work around the conversation, not the conversation itself.

Experience

My background is in B2B SaaS sales, including several years at Pipedrive and now Coverflex, working with Portuguese companies on compensation, benefits and the changing shape of work. Over the years I have sold across different segments and worked with founders, HR teams and commercial leaders trying to make decisions under imperfect information.

I am interested in sales craft, AI-native workflows, decision-making and the systems around good commercial work, but I try to keep the centre of gravity in the right place: tools should make the human part better, not make it disappear.
