01

Bronze Art: photographing what gets handed down

I photograph under the name Bronze Art ↗. My work is classical and documentary, centred on family life, faith, sacraments, and the quiet details that pass between generations. Rather than directing people into theatrical poses, I look for real light, natural gestures and the ordinary dignity of a home or a church.

A good family photograph is made for thirty years from now as much as for next week. The public portfolio, stories and session enquiries live on bronzeart.pt ↗ and on Instagram ↗.

02

Bronze Art Galleries: delivery is part of the photographic work

The software side of this project grew from what happens after the camera is put away: preparing a finished story for the people receiving it. Families need a calm place to read the sequence, download the finished high-resolution JPEGs and choose photographs for a printed album. I need to review the delivery and keep its organisation clear.

I wanted those tasks to belong to the same photographic process rather than a generic file-transfer link. Bronze Art Galleries is the web application I built to run that delivery workflow from end to end.

03

From an ordinary folder to a chaptered story

One of the practical improvements came from my own use of the tool. Creating a gallery and adding its photographs needed to happen together, using an ordinary folder of finished JPEGs. The application prepares those files in the browser, and subfolders can become chapters in the story.

Before publishing, I can review the cover, the sequence, the personal delivery note and the download files. Uploads can be resumed after an interruption. Adding photographs and replacing a complete set are separate actions, so an update can be prepared while the existing delivery remains available.

04

Photographs people can keep, and an archive that stays separate

Clients can view the story, download the final JPEGs and mark an album selection. The uploaded JPEGs are preserved as delivered; changing the chapter order or the presentation does not re-edit those files. Large deliveries are divided into downloadable ZIPs assembled when requested, keeping a substantial delivery manageable at both ends.

Client access has a defined expiry date. Keeping a gallery in the photographer’s internal archive is a separate decision and does not extend client access. Covers and public sharing text are also kept separate from personal delivery notes. Client delivery links and private family galleries are never listed on this project page.
